Zditor has a built-in PDF viewer for reading and annotating. You can draw on pages, mark regions with area highlights that carry a title and a comment, keep sets of annotations apart in layers, and cite highlights in your Markdown notes. Annotations are saved in a separate file, so the PDF itself never changes.

## How annotation data is saved

PDF annotations are never written into the PDF file. Zditor saves them in a separate JSON file in the same folder, named after the PDF:

```text
example.pdf.zditor-pdf-annotation.json
```

Zditor creates this file the first time you annotate the PDF and saves every change automatically. It stores:

- Layers, with their names and visibility
- Strokes, with their page, layer, points, color, width, and stroke settings
- Area highlights, with their page, layer, region, color, title, comment, and a reference to their thumbnail
- For highlights the agent makes from a quotation, the quoted text

<Tip>
  Because the PDF itself is never modified, your annotations are easy to sync, back up, and keep under version control.
</Tip>

## Interface layout

In the desktop app, the PDF viewer has four areas.

### Title bar

The title bar above the PDF holds:

- **Undo** and **Redo** on the left
- **Print**, **Search**, **Bookmarks**, **Layers**, and **Clear Page** on the right

When the tab is narrow, **Bookmarks**, **Layers**, and **Clear Page** move into a **More** menu. While you search, the search field takes the place of these buttons.

### Annotation toolbar

A rounded toolbar floats over the top of the pages. From left to right, it holds:

- **Keep tool active**
- The annotation tools
- Six colors
- The **Width** slider
- **Stroke Parameters**

When the tab is too narrow for all of it, the width slider and then the colors move into the stroke parameters button, which then shows the current color and is called **Brush settings**. If that is still not enough, tools move into a **More** menu, starting with the less frequently used ones such as **Laser** and **Line**. The menu shows each tool's shortcut letter.

### Zoom and page controls

The bottom-left corner holds **Zoom Out**, the zoom percentage, **Zoom In**, and the current page number.

### Left sidebar

**Bookmarks**, **Layers**, and **Search** open a sidebar on the left of the pages. It shows one of:

- The PDF's bookmarks
- Layers, with the list of area highlights
- Search results

Click **Bookmarks** or **Layers** again to close it.

## Annotation tools

Choose a tool in the annotation toolbar, or press its letter.

### Select

Shortcut: `V`. The Select tool selects and moves annotations instead of drawing. A PDF opens with this tool active.

With the Select tool, you can:

- Click a stroke to select it, and `Shift`-click strokes to add them to the selection or remove them
- Drag a selected stroke, or anywhere inside a selection, to move it
- Click an area highlight to show its card, drag it to move it, or drag a corner handle to resize it

Clicking empty space clears the selection.

### Pen

Shortcut: `P`. The pen is for freehand drawing, including handwritten notes, circles, arrows, and arbitrary paths. Choose one of the six colors and set the **Width**.

**Stroke Parameters** shapes each stroke:

- **Pressure sensitivity**: varies the width with pen pressure. It has no effect with a mouse.
- **Smoothing**: softens the outline of each stroke
- **Stabilization**: lets the line lag slightly behind the pen, so quick strokes come out steadier
- **Taper Start** and **Taper End**: narrow the stroke to a point as it starts or ends
- **Cap Start** and **Cap End**: round the start or end of an untapered stroke
- **Reset**: restores the default stroke parameters

<Tip>
  Width and stroke parameters apply to new strokes; existing strokes keep the settings they were drawn with. Zditor remembers your choices as drawing defaults, which you can also set in **Settings → Editor → PDF Editor**.
</Tip>

### Highlighter

Shortcut: `M`. The highlighter is also freehand, but behaves differently from the pen:

- It is semi-transparent, so the text underneath stays readable
- It blends with the page like a fluorescent marker
- Its width does not change with pen pressure

### Eraser

Shortcut: `E`. Drag the eraser across the strokes you want to remove.

- It removes every stroke its path touches, as a whole, including lines and rectangles
- Its size follows the **Width** setting
- Strokes disappear as you drag, and the change is saved when you release
- It works on strokes in every visible layer

<Warning>
  The eraser does not remove area highlights. Delete those from their card, their editor, or the Annotations list in the Layers sidebar.
</Warning>

### Line and rectangle

For more structured markup, use:

- **Line** (`L`) to draw a straight segment from where you press to where you release
- **Rectangle** (`R`) to draw a box outline between two corners

After you draw one, Zditor switches back to **Select** with the new shape selected, so you can move it right away. To draw several in a row, turn on **Keep tool active**.

### Lasso

Shortcut: `S`. Draw a loop around strokes to select them. Every stroke with a point inside the loop is selected. You can then use the selection toolbar or drag the selection, as described in [Selection and batch operations](#selection-and-batch-operations).

<Tip>
  The lasso selects strokes only. It does not select area highlights or the PDF's text.
</Tip>

### Laser

Shortcut: `K`. The laser is a temporary pointer for presentations, teaching, or screen sharing:

- A dot in the current color replaces the cursor and leaves a trail that fades within a second
- Nothing is saved into the annotation data
- Switching to another tool clears the trail at once

### Pan

Shortcut: `H`. Drag to scroll the pages in any direction instead of annotating. This is especially useful after zooming in.

### Keep tool active

Shortcut: `Q`. After you draw a line, a rectangle, or an area highlight, Zditor normally switches back to **Select**. With **Keep tool active** turned on, the tool stays selected. The pen, highlighter, eraser, lasso, laser, and pan tools always stay active.

### Keyboard shortcuts

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `V` | Select |
| `H` | Pan |
| `P` | Pen |
| `M` | Highlighter |
| `A` | Area Highlight |
| `E` | Eraser |
| `L` | Line |

The tool letters and the undo and redo keys work once you have clicked in the PDF viewer, and never while you are typing in a field.

## Area highlights

Area highlights are the most structured annotation type in the PDF viewer: a rectangle on the page with a color, a title, a comment, and a thumbnail of the region.

### Create an area highlight

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch to the Area Highlight tool">
    Choose **Area Highlight** in the annotation toolbar, or press `A`.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Drag a rectangle on the PDF">
    Drag over the region you want to annotate.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a title and a comment">
    When you release the pointer, a small editor opens beside the region. Choose one of five colors (yellow by default) and type an optional title and comment. Pressing `Enter` in the title moves to the comment.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm">
    Click **Confirm** or press `Cmd/Ctrl + Enter` to save the highlight to the active layer. **Cancel** or `Esc` discards it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

After you confirm, Zditor switches back to **Select** with the new highlight selected, unless **Keep tool active** is on. A highlight with a comment shows a small dot in its top-right corner.

### Edit an area highlight

#### With the Select tool

Click a highlight to show its card, with its title and comment. Click **Edit Annotation** (the pencil) or the card's text to open the editor, or **Delete Annotation** (the trash can) to delete the highlight. The Layers sidebar also opens, with the highlight marked in its Annotations list.

#### With the Area Highlight tool

Click an existing highlight to open its editor directly.

#### In the editor

- A new color applies at once
- After changing the title or comment, click **Save** or press `Cmd/Ctrl + Enter`
- **Close** or `Esc` leaves without saving the title and comment
- The trash can deletes the highlight

### Move and resize an area highlight

With the Select tool, drag a highlight to move it. A selected highlight shows a handle at each corner; drag one to resize the highlight.

While the editor is open, eight handles appear around the box:

- Top-left, top, top-right
- Left, right
- Bottom-left, bottom, bottom-right

This allows precise resizing instead of deleting and redrawing.

### Delete an area highlight

You can remove a highlight by:

- Clicking **Delete Annotation** on its card
- Clicking the trash can in its editor
- Clicking **Delete Annotation** next to it in the Annotations list in the Layers sidebar
- Right-clicking it while the Area Highlight tool is active

Undo brings a deleted highlight back.

### Highlight thumbnails

When you save an area highlight, Zditor captures an image of the region and saves it to the location chosen under **Settings → Editor → PDF Editor → Highlight thumbnails**. The same choice is available under **Settings → Integrations → Image hosting & uploads**. The location can be a local folder or an S3-compatible, WebDAV, or GitHub storage that you have set up.

If a highlight is missing its thumbnail, hover over it in the Annotations list in the Layers sidebar and click **Regenerate missing thumbnails**.

## Referencing PDF cards in Markdown

Area highlights are not limited to the PDF viewer. You can insert them into Markdown documents as PDF cards, which is useful when turning reading annotations into reusable notes.

When you type `@` in a Markdown document, Zditor opens a reference list. That list includes:

- The Markdown files in your workspace
- The PDFs you have annotated

When you choose a PDF that has area highlights, Zditor opens a second-level menu containing two kinds of entries:

- **The PDF itself**, which inserts a link to the whole PDF
- **Each of its area highlights**, which inserts a PDF card for that region

A PDF without area highlights is inserted as a link right away. Highlights are listed by title, or by page and comment if they have no title. Keep typing after `@` to search file names, highlight titles, comments, and page numbers (for example, `page 3`).

A PDF card shows the highlight's title, if it has one, and the PDF's file name.

### Typical workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create area highlights in the PDF first">
    Add area highlights to the important regions, ideally with a title or comment so they are easy to recognize in the list later.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Return to a Markdown document and type @">
    This opens the workspace reference list.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose the target PDF">
    Select the PDF in the first-level list to open its second-level menu.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose the full PDF or a highlight">
    Select the PDF itself for a link to the whole file, or a specific highlight for a card that points at that region.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Insert it into Markdown">
    Zditor inserts the link or the PDF card into the current Markdown document.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Click a PDF card inside Markdown

Click a PDF card to select it and open its toolbar. The toolbar connects your writing with the original PDF:

- **ASK AI**: opens the card in the agent, along with the highlight's page, title, and comment
- **The PDF's file name**: opens the PDF, scrolls to the highlight, and selects it
- **Open with Default App**, **Open Containing Folder**, and **Copy File Path**: act on the PDF file (desktop app)
- The page number of the highlight
- **Edit Title**: changes the title this card shows in the note
- **Refresh**: reloads the highlight's details and thumbnail
- **More Options**: shows the highlight's page, title, comment, and thumbnail. Click the thumbnail to view it at full size.

That means a PDF card is not just a static reference. You can write from the summarized note in Markdown and jump back to the source PDF whenever you need to check the original context.

<Tip>
  This workflow is especially useful for paper reading and research notes. The Markdown side keeps a reusable card, while the PDF side keeps the original annotated region.
</Tip>

This pattern is useful when:

- Turning paper highlights into notes
- Keeping the PDF thumbnail and comment context inside Markdown
- Building structured research notes while reading

## Layer management

Layers are one of the core organizational features of PDF annotations. Click **Layers** in the title bar to open them in the sidebar. Every PDF starts with a **Default Layer**.

### Supported layer actions

The layers sidebar supports:

- Clicking **Add Layer** (the plus) to create a layer, which becomes the active layer
- Clicking a layer to make it active. Clicking a hidden layer also shows it again.
- Double-clicking a layer name to rename it. `Enter` confirms and `Esc` cancels.
- Hovering over a layer and clicking the eye to hide or show it
- Hovering over a layer and clicking the trash can to delete it. The last remaining layer cannot be deleted.

<Warning>
  Deleting a layer also deletes the strokes and area highlights stored in that layer. Undo brings them back while the PDF is open.
</Warning>

Below the layers, the **Annotations** list shows every area highlight with its page, layer, title, and comment. Click one to jump to its page.

### What the active layer means

The active layer decides:

- Where new strokes are created
- Where new area highlights are created
- Where pasted strokes go
- Which strokes **Clear Page** removes

The eraser, the lasso, and the Select tool work on every visible layer. If you hide the active layer, the first visible layer becomes active.

### Layer visibility

Each layer can be hidden or shown independently. When a layer is hidden:

- Its strokes and area highlights disappear from the pages
- They are left out when you print
- The data still exists, and the Annotations list still shows the layer's highlights

This is useful for:

- Separating annotation themes
- Splitting personal and team markup
- Temporarily focusing on one annotation category

## Selection and batch operations

After you select strokes with the Select tool or the lasso, Zditor shows a vertical toolbar to the left of the selection.

Available actions include:

- **Delete**
- **Copy**
- **Cut**
- **Paste**, which appears after you copy or cut. It adds the copied strokes to this page, slightly offset, on the active layer.
- **Move to Layer**, which appears when the PDF has more than one layer

You can also drag inside the selection to move the selected strokes as a group.

Copy and cut keep strokes for pasting within the same PDF; they do not use the system clipboard.

### Delete shortcut

With the Select tool or the lasso, if strokes are currently selected, you can press:

- `Delete`
- `Backspace`

to remove them.

## Undo, redo, and clear

### Undo and redo

**Undo** and **Redo** are in the title bar. They step through every change to the PDF's annotations, on every page and layer:

- Drawing, erasing, moving, pasting, and deleting strokes, and moving them to another layer
- Creating, editing, moving, resizing, and deleting area highlights
- Adding, renaming, hiding, showing, and deleting layers
- **Clear Page**
- Changes the agent makes while the PDF is open

The history lasts while the PDF stays open. When you open the PDF again, the history starts fresh.

### Clear current page

**Clear Page** (the trash can in the title bar, or in **More** when the tab is narrow) removes all strokes from:

- The current page, the one shown in the page number at the bottom left
- The active layer

<Warning>
  **Clear Page** affects strokes only. Area highlights and strokes on other layers stay. Undo brings the cleared strokes back.
</Warning>

## Bookmarks, search, and navigation

### PDF bookmarks

If the PDF includes its own outline or bookmarks, click **Bookmarks** in the title bar to show them in the sidebar.

You can:

- Expand or collapse nested bookmarks
- Click a bookmark to jump to its page, which is shown next to its title
- See the current page reflected in the sidebar: its bookmark is highlighted

A PDF without an outline shows **No bookmarks**.

### Search text and annotation comments

Click **Search** in the title bar or press `Cmd/Ctrl + F`. The search field takes the place of the title bar buttons, and the results open in the sidebar.

PDF search does not only search the PDF body text. It also searches the comments of area highlights.

Search behavior includes:

- Results update shortly after you stop typing, and the viewer jumps to the first one
- Search ignores case
- `Enter`, or the down-arrow button, for the next result
- `Shift + Enter`, or the up-arrow button, for the previous result
- `Esc`, or the close button, to close search and its results

### How search results are shown

When a match is found:

- The page marks the matching regions
- The search field shows which result you are on, such as `2/7`
- The sidebar lists every result; click one to jump to it

There are two result types:

- **Text matches** are grouped by page: each page with matches counts as one result
- **Highlight comment matches** are listed one per highlight, with the highlight's color, page, and comment

## Zoom and reading

The PDF viewer supports:

- **Zoom In** and **Zoom Out** at the bottom left
- Clicking the zoom percentage to fit the page to the width of the viewer
- Pinching on a Mac trackpad
- Holding `Ctrl` and scrolling
- Zoom levels from 40% to 400%
- Clicking the page number, typing a page, and pressing `Enter` to jump there

### Print

**Print** in the title bar prints every page of the PDF with the annotations on visible layers. Hidden layers are not printed.

### Dark mode

When Zditor uses a dark appearance, the PDF pages are inverted for more comfortable reading, with light text on dark pages.

<Tip>
  The inversion is applied only to the PDF pages. Annotations are drawn on a separate layer above them and keep their colors.
</Tip>

## Working with the agent

The built-in Native Agent can work with any PDF in your workspace, whether or not it is open in a tab. You can ask it to:

- Read a PDF page by page, along with its outline and your existing highlights
- Find a passage
- Look at a page or part of one, such as a figure, a table, or a scanned page
- Highlight a passage with a title and a comment, underline it, strike it through, or draw a box around it
- Draw freehand, edit or delete highlights, and delete strokes

What to expect:

- New annotations go on a layer named **Agent**, unless the agent is asked to use one of your layers, so you can hide or delete its work in one step
- If the PDF is open, the agent's changes appear at once, and **Undo** takes back each batch of changes in one step
- For each highlight it creates, the agent gets a ready-made PDF card link, so it can cite the highlight in a Markdown note
- Thumbnails for the agent's highlights are made while the PDF is open. For highlights it made while the PDF was closed, open the PDF and use **Regenerate missing thumbnails** in the Annotations list.
- Reading, searching, and looking at pages need no approval. Adding, changing, or deleting annotations follows the agent's **Permission mode**.

To connect the agent to an AI service, open **Settings → AI → Agents**.

## Performance notes

The page list only renders the pages around the viewport:

- The visible pages, plus one page before and one after, are rendered fully
- Other pages are replaced with placeholders that preserve the scroll height

This reduces rendering cost, especially for long PDFs, larger zoom levels, and high-resolution displays.

The first search in a PDF reads the text of every page, so it can take a moment in a long document. Later searches in the same PDF reuse that text.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Where is annotation data stored?" icon="question">
    In a separate file next to the PDF: annotations for `paper.pdf` are in `paper.pdf.zditor-pdf-annotation.json`. Zditor never writes them into the PDF itself.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why are some annotations missing?" icon="question">
    First check whether their layer is hidden in the Layers sidebar. A hidden layer keeps its data, and its area highlights still appear in the Annotations list. Also remember that the active layer only decides where new annotations go; the other layers' data is still there.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why can't search find my handwritten annotations?" icon="question">
    Search covers the PDF's text and the comments of area highlights. It does not search handwriting or highlight titles, and it does not run OCR, so a scanned PDF without a text layer cannot be searched.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What if a thumbnail was not generated?" icon="question">
    Open the PDF, open the Layers sidebar, hover over the highlight in the Annotations list, and click **Regenerate missing thumbnails**. If it keeps failing, check the storage location under **Settings → Editor → PDF Editor → Highlight thumbnails**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens when I delete a layer?" icon="question">
    Deleting a layer removes the strokes and area highlights stored in it. Undo brings them back while the PDF is open. To keep some strokes, move them to another layer first with **Move to Layer**; area highlights cannot be moved between layers.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I undo changes after closing the PDF?" icon="question">
    No. The undo history lasts only while the PDF is open. To take back an agent's work later, hide or delete its **Agent** layer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between an area highlight and the normal highlighter?" icon="question">
    The highlighter draws a freehand, semi-transparent stroke. An area highlight is a structured rectangle with a title, a comment, a color, and a thumbnail. You can move and resize it, find it by its comment in search, and cite it in Markdown as a PDF card.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
